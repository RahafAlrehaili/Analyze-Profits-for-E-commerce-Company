# Analyze Profits for E-commerce Company

Why did revenue grow while profit barely moved? This project traces the gap between growth and profitability for an e-commerce company across 2024–2025, using Excel to clean the data, build a dynamic dashboard, and turn the findings into concrete recommendations.

## Overview

The company's gross profit grew only 1.6% while total expenses grew 3.3% — nearly double the pace. This analysis digs into *why*, tracing the pressure back to rising COGS on the best-selling product, and closes with three data-driven recommendations for improving margins.

## What I did
- Cleaned and transformed raw financial data using advanced Excel formulas to ensure accuracy and consistency
- Built a dynamic financial dashboard (2024–2025) tracking Gross Profit, Total Expenses, and Net Earnings
- Analyzed product-level performance using pivot tables to identify high-growth, underperforming, and margin-risk products
- Delivered data-driven recommendations for cost optimization and product strategy, presented as a professional PowerPoint deck

## Key findings
- Gross Profit: +1.6% | Total Expenses: +3.3% | Net Earnings: +0.3%
- Gross Margin declined 2.2 points (33.9% → 31.7%) as COGS grew faster than revenue
- EBIT/EBITDA stayed nearly flat — confirming the margin pressure came from rising COGS, not operating inefficiency
- Product-level split: one product driving most sales volume with a slight margin dip, one emerging as a fast-growing high-margin contributor, and one in decline

## Recommendations
1. **Protect margins** on the top-selling product — renegotiate supplier costs and optimize logistics
2. **Scale** the fastest-growing, most profitable product
3. **Reassess** the underperforming product — pricing, marketing spend, or repositioning
